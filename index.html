Research

High-order methods for time-dependent PDEs

My research lies at the intersection of numerical analysis and scientific computing. I study the stability, accuracy, convergence, and computational efficiency of high-order methods for time-dependent partial differential equations, especially discontinuous Galerkin discretizations coupled with advanced time integrators.

01

Runge–Kutta DG methods

Stability and error analysis of high-order Runge–Kutta discontinuous Galerkin schemes, including CFL behavior and energy-based estimates.

02

Filtered DG schemes

Fourier analysis and stabilization of RKDG methods through filtering, with emphasis on preserving accuracy while improving stability properties.

03

Advanced time integration

Exponential time differencing, local time stepping, and efficient high-order temporal discretizations for PDE systems with demanding stability constraints.

How my current research fits together

PDE
Time-dependent PDEs Advection, nonlinear hyperbolic problems, and equations with dissipative or higher-order terms.
DG
Spatial discretization Discontinuous and local discontinuous Galerkin methods provide high-order, locally structured approximations.
RK
Time integration Runge–Kutta, filtered RKDG, and exponential time differencing methods advance the solution in time.
λ
Stability & error analysis Fourier, energy, and projection-based analysis quantify stability, accuracy, convergence, and CFL restrictions.
Δt
Efficient simulation The goal is reliable high-order computation with larger stable time steps and reduced computational cost.

Current emphasis: filtered RKDG methods, exponential time differencing RKDG schemes, and nonlinear stability/error analysis.

Additional interests: local discontinuous Galerkin methods, compact stencils, high-order PDEs, Fourier analysis, local time stepping, and nonlinear stability analysis.
